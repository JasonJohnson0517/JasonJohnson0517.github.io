3 - Ethics and Bias in AI

------------------------------------------------------------
Introduction
------------------------------------------------------------
Artificial Intelligence (AI) has rapidly become a foundational technology in
industries such as healthcare, finance, education, law enforcement, and
transportation. While AI systems offer enormous benefits, they also carry
significant risks — especially when decisions affect people’s lives. Issues of
bias, fairness, and ethics are central to how AI should be designed, deployed,
and governed. This paper explores how bias enters AI systems, why fairness is
difficult to achieve, and what ethical principles guide responsible AI use.

------------------------------------------------------------
What Is Bias in AI?
------------------------------------------------------------
Bias in AI refers to systematic and unfair discrimination against individuals or
groups. AI bias typically emerges in one of three main ways:

1. Data Bias
   - AI models learn from historical data. If the data reflects past inequality,
     the model will reproduce those patterns.

2. Algorithmic Bias
   - Even with balanced data, the mathematical structure of a model can amplify
     disparities.

3. Human/Design Bias
   - Developers make choices about what features to include, what data to ignore,
     and how to define “success,” which can embed subjective values.

------------------------------------------------------------
Why Fairness Is Hard
------------------------------------------------------------
Fairness in AI is not a single concept. Different definitions of fairness often
conflict, making it impossible to satisfy all of them simultaneously. Thus,
ethical choices must be made about which fairness criteria matter most for a
context.

------------------------------------------------------------
Real-World Examples of AI Bias
------------------------------------------------------------
- COMPAS criminal justice tool producing higher false positives for Black defendants.
- Amazon hiring algorithm downgrading resumes with references to women's activities.
- Healthcare prediction models mistakenly prioritizing patients based on spending.

------------------------------------------------------------
Ethics Principles in AI
------------------------------------------------------------
Responsible AI involves:
- Transparency
- Accountability
- Privacy
- Fairness and non-discrimination
- Human oversight
- Inclusiveness

------------------------------------------------------------
Mitigating Bias
------------------------------------------------------------
Strategies include:
- Balanced datasets
- Fairness-aware preprocessing
- Algorithmic debiasing
- Post-processing corrections
- Continuous monitoring

------------------------------------------------------------
Conclusion
------------------------------------------------------------
AI offers tremendous opportunities but poses risks when bias is left unchecked.
Ethical AI development requires fairness, accountability, transparency, and
ongoing evaluation to ensure technology benefits society.
